# Editorial playbook

## Retention structure

Identify these beats before editing:

1. Hook: the most surprising concrete claim or tension, ideally in the first 0.5–1.5 seconds.
2. Context: only what the viewer needs to understand the claim.
3. Escalation: examples, contrast, evidence, or stakes.
4. Payoff: the clearest conclusion or useful takeaway.
5. CTA: keep only when it is natural and specific.

Open with spoken content, not a logo animation. Cut breaths only when the result still sounds human. Preserve micro-pauses before punchlines.

Reordering must preserve chronology, attribution, and the strength of the speaker's claim. Keep useful caveats such as a product still being imperfect. Remove repeated examples, irrelevant tangents, long lists, and dead air before removing context that makes the story credible. A strong line late in the recording can become the opening, supported immediately by real proof; remove its later repetition when unnecessary.

For a personal build story, a useful shape is: strongest result → brief personal motivation → failed attempts → what changed → visible examples → honest limitation → one specific CTA. Adapt to the recording; do not force every story into this sequence or invent connective speech.

## Voice and style references

When the creator points to a voice file, writing skill, or past posts, read them and borrow their concrete first-person experience, plain language, specific actions, and honest limitations. Comparing voice does not request any publishing pipeline. Keep the original transcript separate from the edited script and document substantial cuts.

When the creator names a reference account or video for style, inspect accessible representative examples and translate observed pacing, framing, captions, and proof into choices for this recording. If access is blocked, say so and use the stated preferences; never claim to have reviewed unavailable videos. Check current platform/creator guidance when explicitly requested, but do not add a trend that weakens the story.

## Visual rhythm

- Use a medium portrait crop as the anchor.
- Use subtle punch-ins for emphasis (roughly 106–118%, constrained by source quality); avoid repeated alternating zooms on every sentence.
- Reframe toward the eyes and keep them near the upper third.
- Use B-roll full-screen when it provides proof or concrete context. Use picture-in-picture when the audience should retain the speaker's reaction or credibility.
- Article screenshots should highlight only the relevant headline, chart, sentence, or number. Animate a slow push toward the evidence.
- Prefer match cuts and motivated motion. Avoid template-transition packs that compete with speech.

## Caption rhythm

Keep most chunks to 2–5 words and one or two lines. Emphasize one meaningful word per beat using weight or color. Do not color every word. Keep punctuation natural for Hebrew and manually verify right-to-left ordering in rendered frames.

## Review questions

- Would the first frame and first sentence stop the intended viewer?
- Is every retained sentence necessary?
- Does each B-roll insert make the spoken idea faster to understand?
- Can every caption be read once at normal phone viewing distance?
- Are claims and screenshots faithful to their sources?
- Does the ending land cleanly instead of fading after the idea is complete?
